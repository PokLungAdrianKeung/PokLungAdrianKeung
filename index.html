📄 Abstract

📄 Abstract

At present, marine fungi have emerged as a prominent source of these compounds, garnering substantial research interest. Bergamotane sesquiterpenes, a class of sesquiterpenes featuring a 6/4 bicyclic core structure, exhibit a wide spectrum of biological activities, including antibacterial, anti-HIV, and anti-diabetic properties, rendering them valuable candidates for the development of novel pharmaceuticals and biological herbicides. Previous investigations have demonstrated that the fungal strain HDN15-135, isolated from the deep sea of the Indian Ocean, possesses the capacity to metabolize polycyclic bergamotane sesquiterpenes. Consequently, bioinformatics analysis was employed to identify a bergamotane biosynthesis gene cluster within the genome of this strain, which encompasses multi-step post-modifying enzymes.

To elucidate the role of post-modifying enzymes in the biosynthesis pathway of polycyclic frameworks and to facilitate the large-scale production and experimental exploration of their active structural configurations, this study proposes the preparation of heterologous expression strains. By manipulating different gene combination variables and monitoring product formation, the catalytic sequence of the modified genes following the initial two steps in the biosynthesis pathway will be determined. This research endeavor not only lays the groundwork for unraveling the synthesis mechanism of the bergamotane polycyclic skeleton but also offers insights into the diversity of sesquiterpene metabolites within the bergamotane family. Through experimental analysis, the isolated monomeric compounds were subjected to LC-MS and NMR analysis, yielding two compounds. This study aims to systematically identify diagnostic and therapeutic targets intimately associated with the pathogenesis of NASH and liver injury by integrating multi-omics data with advanced bioinformatics methodologies. The core significance of this research lies in employing an in silico strategy to elucidate the causal pathways underlying the disease. This approach is expected to provide a robust theoretical foundation for the development of non-invasive diagnostic tools and the discovery of novel drug targets, thereby advancing precision medicine in NASH.

Methodologically, this study employs Mendelian Randomization (MR) as the primary analytical approach. We integrate multi-tiered data from large-scale population-based Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS), plasma proteomics, transcriptomics, and metabolomics.
